Eleven this year, which is about one a month if I squint. Ordered by when I finished them, not by how good they were. My favourite gets a highlight.

- *A Philosophy of Software Design*, John Ousterhout. Read it for CPSC 310, kept reading it after. "Deep modules" now lives in my head rent-free.
- *Kitchen*, Banana Yoshimoto. My first novel in (assisted) Japanese. Took two months. Worth it.
- *The Mom Test*, Rob Fitzpatrick. Changed how I talk to people about pepper docs.
- *Shoe Dog*, Phil Knight. Read it in two sittings on the Canada Line.
- <mark>*Tomorrow, and Tomorrow, and Tomorrow*, Gabrielle Zevin. The best book I read this year, and maybe the best one about making things with friends.</mark>
- *The Design of Everyday Things*, Don Norman. Finally read the whole thing instead of quoting the door chapter.
- *Klara and the Sun*, Kazuo Ishiguro. Quiet and sad. Read it at the wrong time, during finals.
- *Creative Selection*, Ken Kocienda. Demos over decks. I think about this every time I make slides.
- *How to Do Nothing*, Jenny Odell. Made me delete Instagram for a month. It came back.
- *Norwegian Wood*, Haruki Murakami. Everyone said start here. They were right.
- *Make Something Wonderful*, Steve Jobs. Free, short, read it on my phone.

Up next: *The Pragmatic Programmer* (yes, I know) and whatever `@mina` lends me.

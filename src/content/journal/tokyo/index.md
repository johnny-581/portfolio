Landed at Haneda on `dec 14` with a carry-on, a JR map I never used, and a rule: order everything in Japanese, even when the menu has English.

We stayed in Kōenji, which turned out to be the right call. It has small streets, secondhand shops, and a 7-Eleven every block that was somehow always warm.

![a convenience store glowing at the end of a dark side street](./1.jpg)
<small>the 7-eleven two minutes from our place. i went more times than i'll admit.</small>

Things that went well:
- ordering at a standing soba place without pointing, on day four
- a forty-minute conversation with a bookshop owner in Jimbōchō about *Kitchen*, mostly in Japanese. He was very patient.
- `@kenta` showing us an izakaya with no sign and no English menu, where I understood maybe half of the jokes

Things that did not:
- I said すみません about four hundred times
- I got lost in Shinjuku station twice, the same way both times

The best part was the quiet. <mark>Tokyo is huge and loud, and then you turn one corner and it's a residential street with laundry out and a cat on a step.</mark> I started taking photos of those corners instead of the famous stuff.
